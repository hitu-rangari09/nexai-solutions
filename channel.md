## 2. Audience

*(proposed — correct anything that doesn't match who you're actually talking to)*

- **Who they are:** Indian students who need *full* funding because self-funding a
  foreign degree is not available to them. Mostly final-year undergraduates, recent
  graduates, and early-career people applying for master's or PhD.
- **What they already know:** That these scholarships exist, and usually the name of one
  or two. They have read the aggregator blogs.
- **What they're stuck on:** The gap between "this scholarship exists" and "here is the
  document I am holding, and what do I do with it." Sequencing, paperwork, what counts
  as proof, what the portal actually wants, and what happens after the award.
- **Why they'd click:** Specificity. A named program, a named stage, a real document.
- **Why they'd leave:** Generic advice they've read five times. Vagueness where they
  needed a number or an order of operations. Any smell of being sold something.

## 3. Voice

*(proposed)*

- **Persona:** Someone a year or two ahead of the viewer who kept good notes and is
  walking back to explain the part that confused them.
- **Point of view:** Second person ("you"), with first person only for lived experience.
- **Language:** `<< TO FILL — OWNER ONLY >>` — English, Hindi, or Hinglish? This changes
  scripting, TTS voice selection, and captions, so it is needed before the script stage.
- **Reading level:** Plain English, short sentences. Many viewers are working in their
  second or third language — that is a reason for clarity, never for condescension.
- **Pace:** 145–165 wpm long-form; faster for Shorts.
- **Humor:** Light and dry at most. The viewer is anxious about a life decision.
- **Formality:** Direct and practical. Not stiff, not chatty.

### Voice rules
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Concrete over abstract. Name the program, the form, the document.
- No hype words: *insane, crazy, secret, hack, guaranteed, life-changing, blow up*.
- **No promises about the viewer's results** — never imply the channel improves anyone's
  odds of selection.
- Say "I don't know", "this changed since I applied", or "check the official page" when
  that is the honest answer. On this topic that sentence is the product, not a weakness.
- Separate lived experience from research, out loud: "when I applied…" vs "the current
  page says…". The viewer needs to know which one they're getting.

## 6. Hard limits for this channel

- No copyrighted music, footage, images, clips, logos, or voice likenesses without a
  license or written permission the owner can produce on request.
- **No claims of results, income, or outcomes for the viewer.** Never imply this channel
  improves the odds of selection.
- **No unredacted documents, ever** — the owner's or anyone else's. Passport numbers,
  application IDs, addresses, referee names and contact details, email headers, and
  signatures all get removed before the frame is rendered. A redaction miss is permanent.
- **No other person's documents at all** without their written permission — not a
  friend's award letter, not a screenshot from a group chat.
- No university or government seals, letterheads, or logos beyond what is factually
  necessary.
- **Every deadline, fee, eligibility threshold, and requirement is verified against the
  primary source** (embassy, CSC, MEXT, Mitsui, university) in the same cycle it is
  published — never from an aggregator blog, and never from memory.
- Every such figure is dated on screen and paired with "check the official page before
  you rely on this."
- No medical / legal / immigration / financial advice as fact. Visa and financial
  material is general information with a "confirm with the embassy / a professional"
  pointer.
- No fabricated personal anecdotes presented as real. Lived experience is labeled as
  lived experience; researched material is labeled as researched.
- **Conflict of interest:** if an application service, test-prep company, forex or
  remittance company, or agency ever pays or gifts the channel, it is disclosed in the
  video itself and in the description. Any offer that requires not disclosing is refused.
- Do not name or accuse specific agents or companies of fraud. Explain the mechanics of
  a scam pattern instead.
- AI voice and AI visuals are disclosed. Assume disclosure is required unless the owner
  has personally verified current YouTube policy says otherwise.
- Anything research could not verify is labeled as unverified in the script, or cut.
